# New combinations

After 松本勝『破壊的イノベーションの起こし方――誰でも使えるアイデア創出
フレームワーク』(東洋経済新報社, 2021).

Innovation is a **new combination**: elements never combined before,
combined so that new value appears. Not invention from nothing, and not
a variation on what exists — a variation combines the same things in the
same way, slightly better, and competes on the incumbent's terms.

## The moments this replaces

| About to… | Instead |
|---|---|
| brainstorm variants of an existing solution | name the need and the seed separately, then look for combinations that have not been tried |
| start from an asset and hunt for a use | start from someone's ideal state; the asset re-enters later as one candidate seed among several |
| serve only the needs users say out loud | the voiced needs are where everyone already competes — ask what users do not know to ask for |
| add a feature | integrate: the test is whether the surface got simpler. A capability that complicates the UX was aggregated, not integrated |
| score an idea by novelty | score empathy with the need × feasibility of the solution; novelty without empathy is a curiosity |

## The grid

Two axes: is the **need** manifest (voiced, known) or latent (real but
unarticulated)? Is the **seed** — the technology, data or asset — existing
or new? All four quadrants can innovate, but only through a combination
that is new; the pairs already tried are occupied ground.

| | Existing seed | New seed |
|---|---|---|
| **Manifest need** | new pairing of known parts — cheapest to test, easiest to copy | known problem, new capability |
| **Latent need** | the seed was there all along; the insight is the need | hardest to reach, hardest to copy |

The disruptive weight sits in the bottom row. A latent need, once named,
looks obvious in hindsight — which is why it is found by observing what
people do and work around, not by asking what they want.

## Why before What before How

Design thinking here is backcasting from a person's ideal state: whose
day is being changed, and what does their satisfied state look like? The
Why is decided there. What and How follow, and the conversion habit —
転換思考 — is returning to the Why whenever the discussion has drifted
into mechanisms, because a mechanism argued without its Why is an asset
hunting for a use again.

Structure a candidate as a sentence with all its slots filled — who,
why, what, how — and the empty slot exposes what kind of guess the idea
still is: a Who without a Why is a demographic, not a need; a How
without a What is a technology demo.

## Integration, not aggregation

統合思考: integrating needs while making the UI/UX simpler. Several real
needs, one surface that answers them with less to learn than before.
The check is directional — after the change, is there less for the user
to understand, or more? Growth by accumulation of features fails this
test one release at a time, and each release looks reasonable alone.

## Judging an idea

Two axes, both required: **empathy** — how strongly do the people it is
for recognise the need when shown it — and **feasibility** — can the How
actually be built and delivered. High empathy with weak feasibility is a
wish; high feasibility with weak empathy is a solution in search of a
problem, however novel. Test empathy on people outside the room that
produced the idea; the room always resonates with itself.
