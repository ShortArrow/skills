# New combinations

After 松本勝『破壊的イノベーションの起こし方――誰でも使えるアイデア創出
フレームワーク』(東洋経済新報社, 2021). The book's claim, in one line:
ideas are not awaited, they are **searched** — and the search space is
finite enough to walk.

## The frame

Innovation is a new combination of a **need** and a **seed** (a
technology, data, an asset), yielding a solution. Nothing needs
inventing: a combination of existing need and existing seed qualifies,
as long as the pairing itself is new. Judging a combining mind as "just
gluing existing things together" is the tell of having missed this.

A **latent need** has three equivalent test forms:

- asked "what troubles you?", the user cannot name it
- once experienced, the user will not go back
- it is a wish **unconsciously given up** — filed as a dream, not a demand

Latent needs become manifest the moment they are served (nobody missed
carriages before carriages; everybody did after), which is why the
ladder never runs out. The disruptive quadrants are the latent ones, and
they cannot be reached by interview.

## Needs are generated, not collected

A need does not float free; it fires when **a person with a desire, in a
place, at a moment, tries to do something**. So generate:

    (primal desire) × (scene) = need
    Who × Where × When → Why;  Why × What(seed) → How

Two facts make this a search rather than a hope. Scenes are infinite,
so hunting needs directly is panning for gold. **Primal desires — the
innate ones — number only a few dozen** (Murray's catalogue serves as
the list): fix a desire, sweep scenes, and every generated need is
automatically anchored to something real. Observation, the classic
empathy tool, only samples the corner of the space you can see from the
street; combination walks all of it.

Sharpen a scene with modifiers until it can be *lived* rather than
described — an impatient professional, at a crowded gate, rushing. The
modifier on Who must itself name a primal desire; "plain-looking" is a
feature, not a want, and generates nothing.

## Quality comes from integration

People are greedy: the service that satisfies more needs at once wins,
and one more need than the incumbent is a strategy. But not any needs —
**integrate the ones that currently cannot coexist**. A trade-off is a
resignation nobody voices: "warm means bulky, that's just how it is."
The language is diagnostic:

- **"〜だから仕方ない" (resignation)** — a trade-off lives here; this
  sentence, overheard, is an opportunity
- **"〜なのに〜" (astonishment)** — a trade-off has been resolved; this
  is the sentence users repeat to others, which is why resolved
  trade-offs market themselves

Given one need, iterate over the primal desires looking for the one in
tension with it — thorough teaching × impatience gave AI tutoring. The
practical shortcut: extract the **offered values** of services people
already use daily (their needs are guaranteed by the usage), abstract
*one* side, and import only the values distinctive to it. Home × hotel
= time-share resort; department store × e-commerce = chat fitting.
Integrating without abstraction just puts two shops in one building.

**Integration must simplify.** The test is directional: after the
change, is there less for the user to learn, or more? A concept sentence
fixed early — "a thousand songs in your pocket" — decides which
functions belong; anything off-concept is refused however good. The
failure mode has a name: every user exercises a different 5% of the
features, so nothing can be removed and nobody new can enter.

## Flipping premises

The other refinement tool: write down the **unconscious premises** as
flat sentences — "consoles are for the core gamer on the sofa", "shoes
are symmetric" — then negate each one mechanically: *would it be fine
if not?* Most negations die; the survivors are ground nobody thinks
from, which is what makes them blue oceans (asymmetric soles for
counter-clockwise sports tracks; players who move their bodies).

Flip at the right altitude: negating "John the dog" yields another dog;
climb to "pet" or "living thing" before flipping and you reach robots.
Experience caps imagination — the experts asked to picture the future
of transport described a muscular horse — so the counter is deliberate
abstraction, and outside critique whose job is to ask "must it be ○○?"
rather than to answer. Needs have premises too: "faster, because
travel time is wasted" flips into "travel time worth savouring", a
different product entirely.

## What disruption actually is

A service is not destroyed by a better rival; it is destroyed when the
**reason to choose it disappears**. That happens when a newcomer covers
*all* of its important values, adds values it structurally cannot have,
and stays simpler. Source the additions from services used **for the
same purpose** — those already share a market, so users slide over;
values from a different purpose have no seat to move from. This also
reads in reverse: list what only the incumbent still offers (the laptop
keeps its big screen and typing), and you know exactly what protects it
and what to solve to finish it.

## Judging an idea

Four axes, read in pairs — the need: **empathy** and **unresolvedness**;
the solution: **novelty** and **feasibility**. All four are
time-indexed: a rival's launch deletes novelty, a new seed creates
feasibility, so a verdict is dated, not permanent. Test empathy outside
the room that produced the idea; the room always resonates with itself.

For 0→1, do not open with interviews: the user holds no answer where no
experience exists, and words alone implant the wrong image — prototype
first, then ask. When feedback arrives as "add a ○○ button", extract
the desire underneath and discard the spec; requests implemented
verbatim assemble a déjà-vu product. And state the need concretely
("the ones everyone silently gave up on, realised this way") — a
concrete need shrinks the calculated market and raises the real odds;
sharp and narrow first, widen after.

## Working style

Mass-produce idea seeds first — quality is born from quantity — then
process the survivors: integrate or flip, on the need or on the
solution, in any order. For an online service, design the feedback loop
at planning time: which desire is provoked, which action it triggers,
which data that leaves, which insight the data yields, which improvement
closes the loop — a missing link anywhere stalls the whole cycle, and
retention, not acquisition, is where the economics live. Personalisation
is topping; the primal desire is the dish.
